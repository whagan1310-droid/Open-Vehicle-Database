Oil Pressure Warning Lamp/Indicator: Description and Operation

The low oil pressure indicator light works in two ways: it flashes continuously following a momentary loss of oil pressure, or it goes on and stays on with a complete loss of oil pressure.


With the ignition switch in RUN or START, battery voltage is applied to the low oil pressure indicator light. If you don't start the engine, the closed engine oil pressure switch provides ground and the light comes on. When you start the engine, the alternator provides an "engine running" input to the integrated control unit. With normal oil pressure, the engine oil pressure switch is open and the oil pressure indicator light does not come on. If the oil pressure falls momentarily (switches for more than 0.5 second) but then rises again, the YEL/RED wire at the integrated control unit will sense ground through the switch. The integrated control unit will then

provide and remove ground for the low oil pressure indicator light through the YEL/RED wire. The light will flash on and off until the ignition switch is turned OFF. The flashing feature will not work until 30 seconds after the engine running input is supplied by the alternator. This delay keeps the light from coming on due to low oil pressure just after the engine starts.


If engine oil pressure falls below 4.3 psi and does not increase while the engine is running, the engine oil pressure switch will stay closed. The low oil pressure indicator light will then go on and stay on.
