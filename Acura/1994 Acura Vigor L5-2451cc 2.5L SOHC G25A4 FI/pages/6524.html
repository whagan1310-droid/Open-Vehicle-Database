Low Fuel Lamp/Indicator: Description and Operation

The low fuel indicator light comes ON when the fuel level in the car falls below 3.3 U.S. gallons. The light is a reminder that you must refuel soon.

When the ignition switch is in its ON (II) or START (III) position, battery voltage is supplied to the indicator and a thermistor mounted in the fuel tank. When the fuel tank has less than 3.3 U.S. gallons (about 3.8 Imperial gallons) of gas in it, the thermistor, a temperature sensitive resistor, prevents the light from grounding.

When the thermistor is cool, its resistance is very high. When the thermistor is warm, its resistance decreases. Fuel in the fuel tank transfers heat away from the thermistor fast enough to keep it cool. The thermistor's resistance stays high and the low fuel indicator light does not come ON. When the fuel level drops below about 3.3 U.S. gallons (about 3.8 Imperial gallons) the thermistor is no longer immersed in fuel. Without the fuel to cool it, the thermistor's resistance decreases. Current flows through the low fuel indicator light and the thermistor to ground, and the low fuel indicator light comes ON.
