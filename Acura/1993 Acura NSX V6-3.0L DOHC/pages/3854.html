Air Bag Systems: Description and Operation

! WARNING !

- Do not use test equipment on the yellow SRS wires and connectors in the steering column, console, dashboard, and floor. You could unintentionally set off the air bag(s) or seat belt pretensioners, which could cause injury to you and others.

- When testing or replacing SRS components, follow the precautions and procedures in the Electrical-SRS section of the Service Manual. Improper testing or Installation of SRS components could cause:


- unintentional activation of the air bag(s) or seat belt pretensioners, resulting in personal injury.

- inoperative air bag(s) or seat belt pretensioners, which could lead to the driver's or passenger's Injury or death If the car were in a severe frontal collision.

The supplemental restraint system (SRS) is a safety device which, when used in conjunction with the seat belt (equipped with SRS retractors), is designed to protect the driver or passenger by deploying only when the car receives a frontal impact exceeding a certain set limit.


With the ignition in RUN or START battery voltage is supplied to the SRS unit through fuse 1 and fuse 2. The SRS unit is grounded at G701. The SRS unit monitors inputs from the left dash sensor, right dash sensor and cowl sensors. The cowl sensors are inside the SRS unit. When one or both of the dash sensors and the cowl sensors are activated simultaneously for at least 0.002 seconds, a current path will be completed through the air bag inflator(s) and seat belt pretensioners to ground, deploying the air bag(s) and seat belt pretensioners.


The SRS indicator monitors the system circuitry. The indicator light will come on forabout6 seconds when the ignition switch is turned to RUN or START If the indicator light does not go off after about 6 seconds does not come on at all, or comes on while driving, there is an abnormality in the system which must be inspected.
