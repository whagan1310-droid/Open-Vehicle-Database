Turn Signals: Description and Operation

Battery voltage is supplied at all times through fuse 46 to the hazard warning switch and the turn signal/ hazard relay. When the ignition is in RUN or START, battery voltage is supplied through fuse 5 to the cancel unit. The cancel unit and the turn signal/hazard relay are grounded at G401.


Turn Signals

The turn signals operate with the ignition in RUN or START when the lever is moved to the left or right turn position. The turn signal switch position is input through the cancel unit to the appropriate left or right control in the turn signal/hazard relay. The flasher unit in the turn signal/hazard relay interrupts the output of the left or right control. The output of the relay goes to the front and rear turn signal lights, the turn signal indicator light, and the integrated control unit. The front and rear turn signal and side marker lights on one side and the turn signal indicator then flash.

As you turn the steering wheel the steering shaft cancel cam closes and opens the two cancel switches. The cancel unit counts the number of times the switches close and the sequence in which they close and open. The sequence is reversed when the steering wheel is turned to the straight ahead position. When the cancel unit detects that the original sequence has been completed in reverse, it energizes the lever cancel coil in the turn signal/dimmer switch. The solenoid then returns the lever to the center position, which, in turn, moves the switch contacts to the center position and the lights stop flashing.


Hazard Lights

The hazard flashers operate with the ignition switch in any position. When you move the hazard warning switch to ON, battery voltage is supplied to both the left and right controls in the turn signal/hazard relay. The flasher in the relay turns the left and right controls off and on to interrupt the output of the relay. All the turn signal lights, indicator lights and side markers then flash.
