Radio, Stereo, and Compact Disc: Description and Operation

The stereo sound system consists of or uses the following major components:


Battery voltage is supplied at all times through fuse 56 to the radio/cassette player for memory and power circuits. With the ignition in ACC or RUN, battery voltage is supplied through fuse 4 to the radio/cassette player for audio and tape operation. With the radio/cassette player switched on, the power antenna will receive battery voltage from the radio/cassette player. On LS models, the amplifier receives battery voltage through fuse 54. The amplifier receives both left and right channel information from the radio/cassette player. The amplifier is grounded through G253. The compact disc changer receives power, ground, and control inputs from the radio/cassette player. The disc changer then sends its audio signal back through the radio/cassette player to the speakers.


The steering wheel controls can be used to adjust the volume either up or down. The audio button on the steering wheel serves three functions:

^ If pressed when listening to the radio, the radio will jump to the next station preset in memory.

^ If pressed when listening to the cassette, the cassette will fast forward to the next selection.

^ If pressed when listening to the compact disc, the compact disc will skip forward to the next selection; or if it's the last selection on the disc, to the next disc.
